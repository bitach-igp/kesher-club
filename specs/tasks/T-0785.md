Decision (2026-11-23, coordinator, on the owner's delegation; the owner can reverse it, nothing is deleted so reversal costs nothing):
- KEEP the port (`packages/ports/src/org-idp.ts`), its simulator and contract suite, the WorkOS adapter and the `auth.orgSso` flag. WIRE LATER, behind `auth.orgSso`, in T-0789 to T-0792.
- Reasons. (1) REQ-AUTH-008 is a live requirement (`specs/requirements.md:14`, P2) and the only thing that satisfies "deprovision within 15 minutes" is this port; deleting it would drop the requirement or force a rewrite later, with `pnpm trace` failing meanwhile. (2) ADR-004 chose WorkOS for institution SSO and SCIM, and the contract tests for the adapter already exist; the unbuilt part is only the caller. (3) The flag is off and its trigger is "First institution asks; vendor contract" (`config/flags.json`), so nothing is lost by waiting and nothing is spent until an institution asks. (4) Deleting is cheap to do later and expensive to undo.
- Premise check against origin/main f4c803e (2026-11-23). TRUE: no caller of `startSso`, `handleCallback`, `parseScimEvent`, `listDirectory` or `WorkOsOrgIdPAdapter.verifyWebhookSignature` outside tests and the adapter; no `workos` webhook binding in `app.ts`; `auth.orgSso` is `defaultProd: false`; REQ-AUTH-008 exists. CORRECTED: this card said no card plans the caller. T-0693 ("Institution staff are deprovisioned within 15 minutes by a SCIM call", REQ-AUTH-008, later) does, but it is unbuilt (no `apps/api/src/admin/scim.ts`), and it is designed for a SCIM server on our side (`DELETE /v1/scim/Users/{id}` with a directory bearer). ADR-004 has the institution's IdP push SCIM to WorkOS and WorkOS call us with `dsync.user.*` webhooks, so that shape is wrong for the chosen vendor. T-0693 is marked SUPERSEDED by T-0790. Also: the OpenAPI already has the generic `POST /hooks/{provider}` (`providerWebhook`, pattern `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]{1,30}$`), so mounting `workos` needs NO contract change; only the SSO login (T-0792) adds paths. And no table maps an IdP subject to a staff party (`org.staff` is keyed by `party_id`), so the wire-up starts with a link table (T-0789).
What stays dead until then: `startSso`, `handleCallback`, `parseScimEvent`, `listDirectory`, `verifyWebhookSignature`, the `auth.orgSso` flag (off) and the `org-idp` registry binding (only built when the flag is on, `config.ts:394`). Staff sign-in is unchanged.
What is true TODAY about the security risk (checked in code, 2026-11-23):
- Nobody signs in to the institution surface through SSO, because no SSO login exists and the flag is off. So no staff account exists whose access depends on an IdP, and an IdP-side removal has nothing to deprovision. The gap bites only on the day an institution is promised SSO with a 15-minute deprovision (REQ-AUTH-008) and `auth.orgSso` is turned on.
- A removed institution member is NOT silently kept today IF the institution removes them in Kesher: an institution admin can set `active = false` through `InstitutionTeam.change` (`apps/api/src/institutions/team.ts`, REQ-INS-020), which updates `org.staff`, strips the `org_*` role from the user and writes an audit row; the role leaves their token at the next sign-in (the access token lives 10 minutes, REQ-AUTH-007). That is the compensating control, and it is MANUAL: staff removed only in the institution's own IdP or HR system keep their Kesher staff access until an admin removes them by hand.
- Residual risk, stated plainly: until T-0790 is built and `auth.orgSso` is on, there is no automatic deprovisioning and no 15-minute guarantee. Mitigation: do not offer SSO or promise the 15-minute SLA to any institution before T-0789 to T-0791 are merged and verified on staging; the flag stays off.
