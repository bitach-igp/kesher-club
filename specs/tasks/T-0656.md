## Inputs from the 2026-11-04 meeting documents
Written up from the Nov 1 recording (see `docs/vendors/crowded/README.md`, "Meeting documents, 2026-11-04"); second-hand, so each item is a thing to confirm in the sandbox or in writing, not a fact.
- **Shape of the route.** Shekel wallets with instant in-wallet conversion (via stablecoins), then a local payout ("Masaf") in Israeli business hours, about one business day, Friday and Saturday wait; Citi Israel for institutional payouts. Customers push dollars in to unique account details. The evaluation compares this with our current sweep: the institution's US account → ACH to Kesher's master account → our own FX and payout ports. If the wallet route lands, the sweep step may not be needed for institutions.
- **Limits to price in:** ILS 2,000 a day per account for local money movement (scope unclear: does it cover payouts to Israeli bank accounts?), incoming ACH about $300,000 a month, incoming Fedwire up to $20,000,000 a day.
- **Economics:** float about 2.5% annualized and interchange up to about 1% accrue to the holder; they change the cost per $1,000 comparison and who earns on balances (the platform or the institution). Pass-through costs unknown.
- **Regulatory note (feeds the note for Ezra):** Per the recording (to confirm), Crowded says the model survives scrutiny if each customer funds from its own account, consents at onboarding to conversion and again for each movement, and the platform does not operate as a money transmitter; the holder briefly owns funds that land in its wallet. Kesher has no consent record for this; any adopt recommendation needs one as a follow-up card. Foundation or fiscal-sponsor linkage is, per the recording (to confirm), Crowded's recommended structure for consistent-purpose flows (stipends).
