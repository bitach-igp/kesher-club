# ADR-018: Crowded as the member funding rail (no Stripe)

Status: Proposed, owner to ratify · Raised: 2026-11-11 (Zvi: if there is no Stripe, can Crowded credit a member's wallet, with Crowded keeping its own books and ours matching them?) · Requirements REQ-WAL-002, REQ-WAL-003, REQ-WAL-005, REQ-INS-024, REQ-LED-004 · Cards T-0697 to T-0715 (not contiguous, see Consequences), plus T-0716 to T-0733 added by the 2026-11-11 decision (see its section)

## Context
Today a member's wallet is a ledger shadow (ADR-014 decision 4): a load is a PULL through `FundingPort.createLoad` with a
saved payment method (Stripe is the only vendor adapter), an ACH load is held in `pending` and a worker job
(`ReleaseHoldsJob`) moves it to the wallet on the hold date, and `partner-recon` compares a funding statement to our
journals. Stripe also backs billing (memberships), and `billing.stripe` is a `defaultProd: true` flag.

The owner asked what it would take to drop Stripe and let Crowded (already our institution-banking vendor, ADR-017 and
`docs/vendors/crowded`) be the rail that credits a member's wallet, by ACH and by card where its page supports it, with
Crowded keeping its own books. The answer below is a best guess built from Crowded's published docs
(`docs/vendors/crowded/postman-2sBYArTsS6.md`, "the snapshot") and the 2026-11-04 meeting notes. Nothing here has been
confirmed by Crowded in writing; each assumption says where its evidence is, or that it is unconfirmed.

Crowded's model is a push model: a payer pays a Collection through a hosted page (the Intent's `paymentUrl`), by card
or by bank transfer, and the money lands in Crowded's holding then in the chapter's accounts. It has no ACH debit of a
saved bank account. So the rail changes the shape of a load from "we pull" to "the member pays on a page, we wait".

## Assumptions
Each is a bet. If one is wrong the card that depends on it says so in its `Assumption:` line.

- **A1. A member is a Crowded contact with a `wallet` account.** Each member is a contact in the platform entity's
  chapter and has a Crowded account of product `wallet` (Bulk create accounts). The partner's id is kept as the
  contact's `externalId`. *Evidence:* Bulk create accounts takes `contactId` and `product: wallet | perdiem`, is
  idempotent by `idempotencyKey` and returns `accountCreated: false` for a contact that already has the product
  (snapshot, "Bulk create accounts", about line 3922). *Not supported by the docs:* no contact create, get or patch
  field list has an `externalId` (snapshot "Bulk Create Contacts", "Get", "Patch Contact"); `externalId` appears only
  on transactions and platform events. Treat as **unconfirmed, ask Crowded**. Until answered the mapping is ours:
  our party id to Crowded's contact id and account id in `ops.external_ref`, never a column (CLAUDE.md). Crowded
  matches contacts by mobile, unique within the chapter ("Contact Matching"; a duplicate mobile fails that entry), so
  two members, or a parent and a child, who share a mobile cannot both be contacts in one chapter. Also unconfirmed:
  the product `wallet` is described as "Account for business expenses".
- **A2. Crowded is the KYC authority for its wallet accounts.** We collect the data and pass it on the contact (name,
  email, mobile in E.164, date of birth) and treat the contact and account status as a gate. Our own `KycPort`
  (T1 and T2 sessions, no real provider bound yet) stays the tier gate for app features. *Evidence:* contact create
  takes `firstName`, `lastName`, `mobile`, `email` and optional `dateOfBirth`; contact status is `Active`, `Failed` or
  `Archived`; the account status is `Open`, `Closed` or `Frozen` ("Contacts", "Accounts"). The 2026-11-04 recording
  says light KYC: personal ID, date of birth, email, no photo (README answers log, question 8, second-hand). The
  inventory (section 7, item 17) notes there is no contact verification endpoint, so a `Failed` contact is the only
  rejection signal we can read. *Unconfirmed, ask Crowded:* whether a wallet account needs further KYC steps, and
  whether Crowded onboards members itself through a link.
- **A3. A load is a Crowded collection plus an intent.** One collection per load, with a fixed `requestedAmount`, its
  title carrying our load reference (50 characters at most), claimed by title like the existing institution
  collections (T-0657); then one intent for the paying contact. The payer pays on Crowded's hosted or embedded page
  (`paymentUrl`; an embedded token for the contact), by card or bank transfer. Apple Pay and Google Pay are not
  mentioned anywhere in the docs: assume unavailable. *Evidence:* "Collections" (a fixed collection lets each contact
  pay the exact amount once), "Create Collection" (`title` max 50, `requestedAmount` min 100 cents, no idempotency
  key), "Create Payment Intent" (`contactId`, `requestedAmount`, `payerIp` and `userConsented: true` required,
  `successUrl` and `failureUrl` with `{collectionId}`, `{intentId}` placeholders; an unpaid intent for the same contact
  is updated, not duplicated), "Generate Embedded Token" (short-lived, a new one invalidates the last). A grep of the
  snapshot and the inventory for Apple Pay and Google Pay finds nothing. Two notes from the docs: the intent's
  contact is the PAYER, so when a funder pays for a member the contact is the funder's and the wallet account is the
  member's (the funder needs a contact, not an account); and the REST amount is gross while webhooks carry the net
  (inventory section 7, item 10), with a processing fee (`fee`, `feeCovered`) whose payer we do not know.
- **A4. Settlement.** `collect.payment.succeeded` on a bank transfer starts our existing hold. Hold days are ours:
  Crowded says only "several business days" and that a payment can be `Returned` after `Succeeded`. After the hold
  passes with no `collect.payment.returned`, we call `release-funds` for the amount (held collection money to the
  chapter checking account), then book-transfer chapter checking to the member's wallet account (instant, idempotent
  on a UUID key). For a card we release and book at once on `Succeeded`. Assumes fund holding is enabled for our
  partner (unconfirmed). *Evidence:* "Payment Status Lifecycle" (bank transfers "may be returned even after showing
  Succeeded", "reconcile against later Returned events for a few business days"; "Pending ... can persist for several
  business days"), "Release Funds" (held collection funds to the chapter's deposit account, "Requires fund holding to
  be enabled", `idempotencyKey` optional, auto-generated if absent), "Payments" intro ("When fund holding is
  enabled ... automatic fund release is disabled and partners must explicitly transfer funds"), "Book Payment"
  (chapter checking on one side, contact account on the other, `idempotencyKey` a UUID v4 per payment, at most 10 per
  request, a rejection is HTTP 201 with `status: Rejected`), and the collect payment events ("A successful collection
  payment does not by itself mean held funds have been released"). Release is a pooled amount ("All held funds are
  consolidated"), not per payment. The collect payments listing shows `isReleased`, `releasedAt` and `releaseAccountId`.
- **A5. Returns.** A return before release reverses the hold journal and fails the load (existing behaviour, see
  Findings). A return after release is a platform loss: open an ops case, do not debit the wallet blind. *Evidence:*
  `collect.payment.returned` "can occur after success; update your records to reflect the return" (snapshot,
  "Payment Events"). *Unconfirmed, ask Crowded:* who bears a return after the funds were released (is the chapter
  account debited), and the return window. Our own general knowledge, not Crowded's: an unauthorized consumer ACH
  return can arrive up to 60 days after settlement, so no hold we choose removes the risk.
- **A6. Refunds.** The Crowded API refunds only card payments with status `Succeeded`, within 30 days, no refund
  already pending (snapshot, "Refund" eligibility table). An ACH-funded refund (closure refunds, REQ-WAL-005) is not a
  refund call: it is an outbound ACH credit to a counterparty or a book transfer. *Unconfirmed:* we hold no
  verified bank account of the member to credit (see Open questions).
- **A7. Books.** Crowded is the bank-side record. Reconciliation compares per-member wallet balances (account
  `balance`, `hold`, `available`) and the transaction listing (`collectPaymentId`, `collectRequestId` and
  `collectIntentId` on a transaction trace a credit to its payment) with our journals, plus an aggregate proof: member
  wallets plus pending in our ledger equal Crowded wallets plus held funds. *Evidence:* "Accounts" (balance fields),
  "Transactions" ("Linking to Collect Payments"), "Collect Payments" (cross-chapter listing, filter by `createdAt`,
  `status`, `chapterId`; fields `isReleased`, `releasedAt`, `fee`). **This assumption does not hold as written; see
  Findings, item 4.** *Superseded 2026-11-11 by M6 ("Decision, 2026-11-11"): the owner decided every wallet movement is mirrored, so per-member balance equality is a break. T-0708 is edited and T-0732 added.* The card was first written as the funding-side proof, with balance equality reported as drift.
- **A8. The port.** `FundingPort` is pull-shaped. A hosted flow needs `createLoad` to be able to answer a pending load
  with an `action` (a payment url to open or embed). The Stripe adapter and the simulator stay unchanged for existing
  callers.
- **A9. Billing is out of scope.** Memberships and recurring charges also use Stripe, and its flags default on in
  prod (`billing.stripe`, `billing.applePay`, `billing.googlePay`). Dropping Stripe needs its own decision for billing.
  Crowded payment plans (Installment and Recurring on a collection, "Payment Plans") may cover recurring charges;
  unchecked.

## Decision (proposed)
The flow, if the owner ratifies:

1. **Onboard.** On the first load (or at KYC T1), a `FundingAccountPort` call ensures the member's contact and wallet
   account in the platform chapter, idempotent by our party id, with the contact data passed through. Ids go to
   `ops.external_ref`. A `Failed` contact or a `Frozen` account refuses loads for that member (`FEATURE_OFF`-style
   refusal naming the status, like ADR-017's gate).
2. **Start a load.** `POST /loads` with method `hosted` (the member chooses card or bank on the page). The workflow
   (`runLoad`) is unchanged: `begin`, `submit`, then `pull` calls `createLoad`, which makes the collection and the
   intent and answers `requires_action` with `action: { type: 'payment_page', url }`. The response carries the
   action; the app opens it (or embeds it). The transfer stays `submitted`.
3. **The member pays.** Crowded sends `collect.payment.pending | processing | succeeded | failed | canceled |
   returned | refunded` in batches to `/webhooks/crowded`. Every event is a hint: the handler re-reads the payment
   (`getLoad`) and hands the current state to the load service, as the institution fee path does (T-0652).
   `collect.payment.failed` is also emitted for ACH cancellations and returns "for compatibility", so a `failed` event
   alone never decides; the re-read does.
4. **Hold or credit.** A card `Succeeded` credits at once; a bank transfer `Succeeded` posts the existing hold journal
   (`partner_settlement -> pending`) with `holdUntil = succeeded date + our hold days` (config, no default).
5. **Release.** On the hold date the existing release job asks the vendor again, and if the payment is still
   `Succeeded` the load service first asks the port to move the money (`release-funds`, then the book payment into the
   member's wallet account, each under a key derived from the workflow id), and only then posts the release journal
   (`pending -> wallet`). A repeat finds its own work: both vendor calls and the journal are idempotent.
6. **Return.** Before release: the existing `fail()` reverses the hold. After release: a case `load_returned_after_release`,
   severity high, with the load, the amount and the member; the wallet is not touched.
7. **Closure refunds.** A card load younger than 30 days is refunded through the refund call; any other load cannot be,
   and the closure reports it as needing a payout and opens a case, as it already does for money with no load behind it.
8. **Books.** The nightly `partner-recon` gets a Crowded statement source (collect payments and transactions), and a
   funding-side balance proof compares what we credited with what Crowded moved (A7 and Findings, item 4).

### What changes in `FundingPort`
- `FundingMethod` gains `hosted`. The request side may be `hosted`; a `Load` read back after payment carries the actual
  `card` or `ach`.
- `createLoad` input gains an optional `hosted: { payerIp, userConsented: true, successUrl?, failureUrl? }`; Crowded
  requires `payerIp` and consent on the intent.
- `Load` gains an optional `action?: { type: 'payment_page'; url: string; expiresAt?: string }`. The existing
  `requires_action` status and `clientSecret` (Stripe's payment sheet) are unchanged; a Stripe load never sets `action`.
- A second optional step, `creditWallet(input, idempotencyKey)`, on a separate interface (as `FundingStatementPort`
  is): moves vendor-held money into the member's vendor wallet account before our release journal. A vendor with no
  such step (Stripe) does not implement it and the load service skips it.
- Member accounts are a separate interface, `FundingAccountPort` (ensure contact and wallet account, read status and
  balance), implemented by the same Crowded adapter instance and resolved from the `funding` binding by checking the
  method exists, as T-0684 does for `listSettled`. This avoids a new registry capability, catalogue row and
  provider key. It is a choice, not a requirement; a separate capability is the alternative.
- `parseWebhook` stays on the port for Stripe. Crowded delivers batches, so the Crowded funding adapter does not use
  it: events arrive through the existing `/webhooks/crowded` receiver (one registration, one signature, one secret
  pair), and the generic `/hooks/funding` route refuses Crowded deliveries as `/hooks/crowded` does today.

## Findings from the code (claims in the brief that needed correcting)
1. **A8 is partly built.** `LoadStatus` already has `requires_action`, `Load` already has a `clientSecret`, and
   `applyVendorState`, `LoadView`, the OpenAPI `Load` and the mobile Add money model (`add-money.model.ts`) already handle
   `requires_action`, but only as a Stripe payment sheet secret. What is missing is a generic `action`, a `hosted`
   method and the payer consent fields.
2. **The existing Crowded webhook receiver is gated by `institutions.usBanking`** (off by default) and its `collected`
   handler is chapter-granular, for institution fees. Member funding needs its own flag gate (`wallet.enabled`) and a
   dispatch by chapter (the platform member chapter versus an institution's), or the member events would be refused
   whenever US banking is off.
3. **`PROVIDER_FUNDING` binds funding and billing together** (T-0041). A `crowded` value binds funding only, so billing
   stays on the simulator, and with `billing.stripe` `defaultProd: true` the T-0043 guard
   (`assertNoLiveFlagOnSimulator`) refuses to boot production. Switching those flags off needs the owner's say-so
   (AGENTS.md). It is the same fact as A9, and card T-0709 makes the refusal explicit instead of hiding it.
4. **Balance equality (A7) cannot hold per member, and cannot hold in aggregate, until every wallet movement is
   mirrored at Crowded.** *(2026-11-11: the owner decided to mirror them all, so this finding is now the work list; see M6 and the inventory.)* Our ledger moves a wallet balance for P2P sends between members (T-0505), card spend, FX
   conversion and payouts. Crowded sees none of them: a book payment must have the chapter checking account on one side,
   so a member-to-member send would be two book payments through checking, and card spend, FX and ILS payouts settle on
   other vendors. A member who received a P2P send has a larger ledger balance than the Crowded wallet. What we can prove
   is the funding side: every credit we posted from a load has a Crowded payment, a release and a book payment, with
   equal amounts, and every refund has its Crowded counterpart. Balance (`balance`, `hold`, `available`) is compared and
   reported as drift, not as a break. Mirroring the other movements, or deciding that Crowded wallets are cash
   custody only and the ledger is the member's balance, is the real decision (Open question 1).
5. **The contact has no `externalId` in the docs** (A1), and contacts are unique by mobile within a chapter.
6. **Crowded's sandbox has no simulator for collect payments.** "Simulate Payment Status" works only for payments we
   originate (ACH and wire). A hosted collection payment cannot be driven to `Succeeded` or `Returned` unattended, so
   the gated sandbox suite covers contacts, accounts, collections, intents, book payments and listings, and the payment
   lifecycle stays on the fake (README question 15 already notes this).
7. **Crowded is USD and US-addressed only** (inventory section 7, item 19). A shekel purse is funded as today: the load is
   priced in the instrument currency (T-0517) and `partner_settlement` is posted in USD, so the aggregate proof compares
   in USD through the transfer's conversion legs.
8. **A refund against the member's source has no counterpart for ACH.** `PayoutPort` is Masav (ILS). The outbound ACH
   credit in A6 would go through a counterparty at Crowded, and we do not hold a verified US bank account for a member.

9. **Auto-reload and the agent load intent need a saved payment method to pull.** REQ-WAL-004 (auto-reload) and
   T-1009 (an approved load intent runs the member's load) both call `createLoad` with no member present. Crowded has
   no saved method and no ACH debit, so on this rail neither works: a hosted load always needs the member on the page.
   The card for the app (T-0715) leaves auto-reload Stripe-only; whether it is dropped or Crowded adds a saved method is
   a decision for the owner and a question for Crowded.
10. **There is no way to read one load back.** `POST /loads` is the only loads operation, so the app has nothing to
   poll while a hosted payment is pending. T-0714 adds `GET /loads/{id}`.
11. **Terraform has no Crowded wiring at all** (secrets, `PROVIDER_INSTITUTION_BANKING`): only the docs mention it.
   T-0709 adds the part funding needs.

## Decision, 2026-11-11: mirror every wallet movement

The owner answered Open question 1 (what moves at Crowded for non-load wallet movements) on 2026-11-11, verbatim:

> "we need to mirror all of our wallet movements with Crowded so that the user has immediate access to the money and settlement within the day in the bank record"

So: ALL of them, not "cash custody only". Status of this ADR stays Proposed (the funding rail itself is still the owner's to ratify, and the
decisions D1 to D5 below are open). What follows is an interpretation, tested against the code at `17bace5` and the snapshot
(`docs/vendors/crowded/postman-2sBYArTsS6.md`, "the snapshot"). Each assumption is marked **confirmed** (the docs or the code say it),
**partly confirmed**, or **unconfirmed** (ask Crowded or the owner). Nothing was tried against Crowded's sandbox: the README notes that book
payments and the sub-account fund call are "not tested", so every book-payment statement here is from the published docs only.

### Assumptions (the interpretation)

- **M1. Our ledger stays the real-time member-facing record; every journal that changes a mirrored member's USD wallet or pending balance has a
  matching Crowded movement the same day.** *Partly confirmed, and two corrections.*
  - *The rule is net delta, not per kind.* A journal changes a member's Crowded `balance` by the net change of that member's USD `wallet` plus
    `pending` (excluding load holds, see M3). A hold inside one member (`wallet -> pending`, kinds `p2p_hold`, `auth_hold`, `auth_release`, the
    reversal of a hold) nets to zero and moves nothing at Crowded. A negative net is a book payment member account to chapter checking; a
    positive net is checking to member account. One planner over the postings covers every kind in the inventory below, including reversals.
  - *Correction: "through the existing outbox" does not fit as built.* Evidence in `apps/api/src/transfers/outbox.ts` and
    `apps/worker/src/jobs.ts:195`: (1) `ledger.post()` enqueues nothing; outbox rows are written by transfer state changes, add-on orders and
    allowance runs, in other transactions than the journal, so a crash between the two loses a mirror; (2) the one sink is the outbound-events
    service, which ignores other messages; (3) `publishBatch` calls the sink INSIDE its claim transaction (row locks held across a vendor HTTP
    call, up to 100 messages in series), so a slow Crowded would stall institution webhooks too; (4) a failing row is retried every 10 seconds
    forever: `attempts` is counted but nothing reads it, there is no backoff, no due time and no dead-letter state. Recommendation: a dedicated
    queue table written in the same transaction as the journal (a new `onPosted` hook on `PostgresLedger`), drained by its own worker job modelled
    on `outbox-publish` (claim with `skip locked`, call outside the claim, per-item backoff, a dead-letter state that opens an ops case). The
    existing outbox is untouched.
  - *Correction: Design A is what ADR-014 decision 4 and SEQ-06 already say* ("the book transfer arrives as a port call inside the workflow,
    before the journal, with SEQ-06's compensation"). It was written for a card processor nobody has chosen. Choosing Design B changes a decided
    line; this section supersedes ADR-014 decision 4 for P2P only if the owner picks B (D1).
  - **Design B (recommended): ledger first, mirror after.** The journal posts, the mirror legs are queued in the same transaction and drained
    with idempotent keys (`{journalKey}:m{n}`, a UUID derived from it) under a same-day SLA (`MIRROR_CUTOFF_UTC`). The member has the money at
    once, which is what "immediate access" asks for, and a Crowded outage does not stop the app. Cost: for a while the books differ (bounded by the
    cap in M7), a refused leg is repaired after the fact, and the checking account must carry float.
  - **Design A: Crowded first, synchronous.** The send workflow books the leg(s) at Crowded, then posts the journal. Trade-offs. *Outage
    coupling:* every send, fee, purchase and card clearing fails or hangs while Crowded is down (P2P and cards would need a cap-free "degraded"
    mode, which is Design B by another name). *Latency:* two sequential Crowded calls (about two round trips) on the send path; the JIT card
    decision has a 300 ms p99 budget (REQ-CRD-003) and cannot wait for a vendor, so cards are Design B whatever is chosen. *Compensation:* a
    failed second leg must undo the first (book it back) before any journal exists, which is clean, but a crash between the legs, or an
    unreadable answer, leaves money at checking with no journal and needs a read-back by key. *Books:* equality holds at every instant. A is
    simpler to prove and slower and less available; B is faster and more available and needs the cap, the queue and the repair path.
  - **Recommendation: B for everything, with A's check kept for the one case where it is cheap: a send is refused up front when either side's
    Crowded account is not `Open`.** The owner picks (D1).
- **M2. A wallet-to-wallet movement is two legs through checking, which nets to zero, and needs partial-failure handling.** *Confirmed in the docs.*
  "Each book payment must have the chapter checking account on one side"; counterparties are a Wallet or PerDiem account "within the same chapter"
  (so all mirrored members must be in one chapter, `CROWDED_MEMBER_CHAPTER_ID`); up to 10 payments per request; "continue-on-error ... partial
  success is possible"; per-payment results `success | failed` with `paymentStatus` `Pending | Sent | Rejected`; error codes `NOT_FOUND`,
  `VALIDATION_ERROR`, `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, `ACCOUNT_UNAVAILABLE` (frozen, closed or blocked), `DUPLICATE_PAYMENT`, `PAYMENT_FAILED`; `idempotencyKey`
  "UUID v4", scoped to the chapter (snapshot, "Book Payment", line 10654). What it costs, per send between two members:
  - Two book payments, two keys, two Crowded transactions (each appears twice in `finance.transaction.created`, one event per leg, so four events).
  - *Can the two legs be ONE request?* Yes by the docs (two items in `payments[]`), but items are independent and the order of execution is not
    documented, so leg 2 (checking to recipient) could run before leg 1 (sender to checking) and fail on `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` at checking, or,
    worse, succeed on float while leg 1 fails. Therefore send leg 1 alone, read the result, then leg 2. A pair in one request is a question for
    Crowded (README question 40), not an assumption.
  - Checking carries float for the gap between leg 1 and leg 2, and for any journal whose second side is not a member (fees, add-ons, allowances).
  - A refused leg 2 after a successful leg 1 leaves the sender's money at checking while our ledger says it is the recipient's. Under Design B
    the repair is: retry (outage, `PAYMENT_FAILED`); for a permanent refusal (`ACCOUNT_UNAVAILABLE`, `NOT_FOUND`) compensate leg 1 back to the
    sender AND post a reversing journal for the send, only while the recipient still holds the amount, else open a case. Under Design A the
    repair is just the compensating book payment, no journal.
  - Existing code is NOT this call: `InstitutionBankingPort.bookTransfer` (`packages/adapters/src/crowded/institution-banking.ts:474`) posts to
    `/chapters/{id}/subaccounts/fund`, parent to sub-account only. The member mirror needs a new port and the `POST /chapters/{id}/payments`
    `type: book` call (T-0718, T-0719). Also check key shape: `uuidFromKey` (`map.ts:105`) makes a version 5 UUID and the docs say "UUID v4";
    the gated sandbox suite must prove Crowded accepts it, else the adapter derives a v4-shaped one.
- **M3. "Immediate access" conflicts with A4 (ACH funds are held until they clear). NOT DECIDED HERE. The owner must pick (D2).** *Confirmed
  conflict.* A bank transfer can be `Returned` after `Succeeded`, "for a few business days" (snapshot, "Payment Status Lifecycle"), and
  nothing says who bears a return after release (README question 34, still open). `Pending` can last several business days, so for an ACH load
  the collected money does not exist at Crowded when the member would be given access: `release-funds` can only move money that was
  collected, so any access before `Succeeded` is a book payment from checking, the platform's own float. Options:
  1. **Keep the hold for ACH; instant access for card only (default if the owner does not choose).** Exposure: card chargebacks (outside this
     ADR, same as today). ACH exposure zero. Fails safe. A card load credits at once (`release-funds` then book payment on `Succeeded`).
  2. **Instant access up to a per-member limit, the platform float bearing returns, with a reserve.** Exposure per member is the limit; total
     exposure is the sum of limits in use, bounded by a platform cap and backed by a reserve in checking sized from the return rate. Needs the
     limit policy, the reserve, a recovery path (a returned ACH makes the wallet negative: who chases it) and the answer to question 34.
  3. **Instant access for every ACH load, with recourse to the member.** Exposure is unbounded in amount (an ACH return can arrive up to 60
     days after settlement, our own general knowledge, not Crowded's) and the recourse is a negative wallet we cannot collect on a closed
     account. Not recommended without a legal view.
  *Recommended default, failing safe:* option 1. Until D2 the ACH branch of T-0704 and T-0705 stays as written (hold, then credit) and T-0733 is blocked.
- **M4. Crowded is USD only, so the ILS purse, FX conversions and Masav payouts CANNOT mirror yet.** *Confirmed.* Every snapshot amount is "cents
  (USD)" (line 3920); neither Postman view has an ILS, FX or wallet-in-another-currency call (README, sandbox check 2026-11-10: `/fx`,
  `/exchange-rates`, `/payments/quotes/ils` answer `Cannot GET`); the program proposal lists "ILS wallets and local payouts" as NEXT, "dates
  confirmed at signing" (README, question 6); the adapter already refuses non-USD (`requireUsd`). This is an explicit gap, not a hidden one:
  - The rail is USD only. The mirror port refuses any non-USD amount (`VALIDATION_FAILED`), the planner classifies a non-USD posting as
    `excluded_non_usd` and never queues it, and the job reports the excluded totals per currency every day, so the ILS purse is visible as
    "ledger only".
  - A journal with one USD wallet leg and one ILS wallet leg (an FX conversion, a cross-currency send, a load into the shekel purse, a card
    authorisation paid from the other purse) mirrors its USD leg only. This is necessary, not optional: the USD wallet balance has to equal
    Crowded's. The platform's USD (`fx_position`) stays at checking and backs the ILS liability, which our ledger alone records.
  - The whole rail sits behind `wallet.mirror` (new flag, default false, `config/flags.json`) and fails closed: with it on and the Crowded
    binding or any required key missing, boot refuses (T-0709, T-0724).
  - Open to the owner (D4): while ILS cannot mirror, either the ILS purse stays ledger-only (recommended; the shekel balance is backed by
    platform USD and other vendors, listed here as the gap) or it is switched off for mirrored members. Revisit when Crowded ships ILS (README question 44).
- **M5. Card spend.** *Confirmed from the code and docs: the existing code assumes an issuer that is NOT Crowded.* Who issues is open: ADR-006
  is "Vendor: _pending_" and the card epic was built against `CardIssuingPort` and its simulator (`packages/ports/src/card-issuing.ts`). The code
  is a just-in-time model: the issuer asks OUR API for each authorisation (`apps/api/src/cards/jit.ts`, REQ-CRD-003, a 300 ms p99), we post
  `auth_hold` (`wallet -> pending`) before answering approve, and a `authorization.cleared` webhook posts `card_clearing` (`apps/api/src/cards/settlement.ts`).
  Crowded cannot be that issuer as the docs stand: its cards are debit cards on a deposit account that approve natively ("Funds are held against the
  available balance"), `finance.authorization.*` events are notifications and not a decision request, and the API has no spend-control, PIN or
  activation endpoint (README, "Cards", inventory section 7, items 20 to 23), so funder controls (REQ-CRD-005), the plan cap, velocity and agent
  purchase approvals cannot be enforced. Two paths:
  - **Mirror (the code's path; recommended).** The issuer is external; at clearing our ledger debits the wallet, and the mirror books member
    account to checking (planner, T-0725). Checking then funds the issuer's settlement (a platform-level ACH or wire, a later card with ADR-006).
    A stand-in approval that overdraws the wallet (`overdrawn`, invariant 5) cannot be booked in full at Crowded (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`): the part
    above the Crowded balance is exposure and a case, never a retry loop.
  - **Ingest (only if Crowded issues).** A Visa debit on the member's `wallet` account settles natively at Crowded; our ledger must ingest
    `finance.authorization.created|declined|canceled` and `finance.transaction.created` (`purchaseTransaction`, `cardId`, keyed
    `(transactionId, accountId)`) and post the hold and clearing from them, the reverse direction (T-0726). Our own limits would not apply. Blocked
    on D3 (who issues) and on ADR-006.
  Which one the code assumes: mirror (an external JIT issuer). The Crowded cards stay "possible for digital per diem cards, no spend rules we can
  set" (README).
- **M6. A same-day proof: end-of-day reconciliation, with per-member balance equality a break, no longer drift. This SUPERSEDES the A7 finding
  that balances cannot match** (A7 and Findings, item 4 are amended below). *Follows from the owner's decision; the accounts API is partly
  confirmed.* At the cutoff (`MIRROR_CUTOFF_UTC`) the nightly job proves, with every amount a `bigint` in USD cents:
  1. *Coverage.* Every journal that touches a mirrored member's USD account is classified: mirrored (all its legs `settled` at Crowded),
     internal (nets to zero), excluded (non-USD), or MISSING. A missing or unsettled leg after the cutoff is a break (case `mirror_missing`).
  2. *Per member.* `ledger wallet + pending (USD), less load-held pending, less the member's still-unmirrored items` equals the member's Crowded
     account `balance`. A difference is a break (case `mirror_balance_break`), not drift. `hold` and `available` are not compared: we cannot place
     a hold at Crowded (no endpoint; holds appear only with card authorisations), so our `pending` for P2P and authorisation holds has no Crowded
     counterpart, and a held ACH load sits in Crowded's collection pool, not in the member's account.
  3. *Held loads.* Load-held pending per member equals that member's `Succeeded`, unreleased collect payments (the listing's `isReleased`).
  4. *Aggregate.* Crowded's total USD (chapter checking, every member account, the collection pool) equals the net debit of our USD
     `partner_settlement`; the checking account alone equals the platform's USD claims (`fee_income`, `addon_payable`, `fx_position`,
     `institution_master` for money held at checking) within the tolerance of items in flight.
  Unconfirmed: that the accounts list returns `balance` for every member in one paged read (rate limits unknown, README question 41) and that
  collect payments carry the contact so held money can be attributed per member. T-0707 and T-0708 are edited and T-0732 added.
- **M7. Outage behaviour.** *Follows from Design B.* Mirror items queue; wallets keep working until the unmirrored exposure reaches a cap per
  member and in total, then new money movements fail closed with `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` (503, a closed-set code). Configuration, no default, so
  boot refuses without them (the "no fallback" rule): `MIRROR_MAX_UNMIRRORED_MEMBER_MINOR`, `MIRROR_MAX_UNMIRRORED_TOTAL_MINOR` (USD cents,
  positive integers), `MIRROR_CUTOFF_UTC` (`HH:MM`), plus `CROWDED_CHECKING_ACCOUNT_ID`. The cap counts unmirrored debit-leg and credit-leg amounts
  together (absolute). It applies only to journals that START a movement (`p2p`, `auth_hold` approvals, `fee`, `addon`, `fx`, `allowance`, `payout`);
  it never blocks a journal that records something already done in the world (`card_clearing`, a load settling, a reversal, a repair), or the
  ledger would stop recording facts: those post and raise an alert. The exact table is in T-0724.
- **M8. Where an institution's money physically sits.** *Unconfirmed, new.* `fee` and `allowance` move money between a member wallet and
  `institution_master`. The institution's USD reaches us by an ACH or book transfer into "Kesher's US collection account"
  (`apps/api/src/institutions/us-funding.ts:6-8,306`). If that account is the member chapter's checking, both are one leg through checking. If
  it is in another chapter, a book payment cannot cross chapters ("within the same chapter") and an extra hop is needed (an ACH to an internal
  counterparty "may execute as a free instant transfer", snapshot, "ACH Payment"). Ask Crowded (question 45). T-0727 is blocked on it.
- **M9. Cut-over.** *Unconfirmed, new.* Equality needs both sides to start at zero. `wallet.enabled` is off in prod, so there should be no
  balances yet; `wallet.mirror` may only turn on while every USD wallet and pending balance is zero, or after an opening-balance step (out of
  scope here, a case in T-0732 refuses a non-zero start).
- **M10. A Crowded account exists for every mirrored member.** *Follows from A1 and T-0698.* The member's Crowded wallet account is created at the
  first mirror-enabled journal at the latest. A `Failed` contact, a `Frozen` or `Closed` account makes the member unmirrorable: their movements
  are refused up front (T-0723), not queued.
